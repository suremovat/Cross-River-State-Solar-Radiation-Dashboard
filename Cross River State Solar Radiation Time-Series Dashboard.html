Phase 1

Business understanding

Objective: assess the solar potential for investment in solar microgrids in Cross River State.

Context: only 47% of communities in Cross River State have electricity access and only 38% are connected to the national grid (as reported by Business A.M), so there is a large energy gap and demand is very high.

Question: how much electricity can solar generate, forecast on a monthly basis, for supply to be reliable and cost-effective? Past data and machine learning are used.

Scope: reliability is judged from the forecast range. Cost-effectiveness and demand need cost and load data, which are not yet included.

Success criteria: monthly forecast error under 10% on data the model has not seen; a forecast range reported alongside each expected value; results expressed as PV energy per kWp so they can be used for sizing.

Phase 2

Data understanding

CAMS Radiation Service daily irradiation, 1,826 days from Jan 2021 to Dec 2025, for one point (5.82°N, 8.48°E). No missing or duplicate days.

Clear seasonal cycle: peak around Jan to Mar, lowest in Jul and Aug. Annual totals are stable year to year.

Phase 3

Data preparation

Removed the metadata header, fixed the delimiter and built a proper date. Converted Wh/m² to kWh/m²/day. Added year, month, season (dry Nov-Mar, wet Apr-Oct) and clearness index (GHI ÷ top-of-atmosphere).

Phase 4

Modelling

Gradient Boosting (scikit-learn) on daily GHI using seasonal features, plus 10th and 90th percentile models for the daily range. PV energy = GHI × 1 kWp × 0.75, forecast for Jan 2026 to Dec 2027.

Iteration: a first version with a time-trend variable forecast about 14% too low. It was removed after evaluation.

Phase 5

Evaluation

Backtest on 2025 (trained on 2021-2024): monthly averages within 5.8%. Daily R² is 0.27, so day-to-day weather is not predictable, but the seasonal pattern is.

Compared with a seasonal average (about equal) and Ridge regression (slightly better in year-by-year cross-validation, 0.964 vs 1.000 RMSE).

Against the success criteria: met. Monthly error was 5.8% and a range is reported for every month.

Against the business objective: the forecast shows how much solar energy to expect each month and how much it varies, which supports a first assessment of solar potential. It does not yet show whether a microgrid is cost-effective or meets demand; that needs load and cost data.

Out-of-sample check on 2026 data

The forecast was built from 2021-2025 data only. It was compared with actual CAMS data for the same site (5.82°N, 8.48°E) from 1 January to 14 October 2026: 287 days the model never saw. October is a partial month (14 days) and is left out of the monthly error summary. In the chart and table, a positive error means the forecast was above the actual.

What this means

Success criterion: met on data the model did not see. The 10% monthly target was set after the 2025 backtest, so this 2026 check is the stronger test. Seven of the nine full months were over-forecast, but only slightly, and the dry-to-wet seasonal shape was captured throughout.

Daily accuracy is limited: daily R² of 0.25 matches the 2025 backtest (0.27). The model captures typical output for the time of year, not individual days of weather, so monthly is the appropriate resolution.

The forecast range is slightly narrow: it covers 75% of days when it is meant to cover 80%. This matters if the range is used to judge supply reliability.

The site matches only approximately: this file's altitude is 132 m against 120 m in the original data, so the two grid points may differ slightly. This is unlikely to affect the result.

Phase 6

Deployment

This dashboard, a Google Colab notebook that reproduces the model, and forecast files (daily and monthly CSV).

Maintenance: retrain once a year when new data is released and re-check the monthly error against the success criteria.

Reading the results for microgrids

Key finding and limitations

Limitations: one location; horizontal irradiation only (no panel tilt, temperature, dust or system losses); performance ratio of 0.75 is assumed; the forecast assumes a stable climate; no demand, load or cost data. Next steps: add demand data, more sites, and storage sizing for the wet season.

Notes. Source: CAMS Radiation Service (SoDa), daily irradiation converted to kWh/m²/day. GHI = global, BHI = beam and DHI = diffuse horizontal irradiation. Clearness index = GHI ÷ top-of-atmosphere irradiation. Cloud loss = shortfall of GHI against clear-sky GHI. Dry season = Nov to Mar, wet season = Apr to Oct. Monthly charts use monthly averages, the clearness histogram uses daily values, and the year × month matrix always shows all years.
